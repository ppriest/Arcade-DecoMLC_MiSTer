MAME 0.289 (-nodrc), deco_mlc.cpp, avengrgs. scripts/sprite_load.py: the sprite list, the IRQ
and clip blocks and the DMA'd top of VRAM dumped at every vblank start (scripts/mame/sprdump.lua),
the whole VRAM once at the end, 1800 frames per run, same runs and stimulus as
write_timing_mame.txt (attract --skip 600; play --coin 600 --skip 1500). Counts per visible
line (240 a frame), geometry from draw_sprites() (deco_mlc_v.cpp 144-533) including clip
windows, zoom, raster offsets, 8bpp pairs and auto-flicker.

  cand    entries enabled, inside their Y clip, whose block covers the line: one index-block
          read each (16 B VRAM or 8 B gfx2 ROM)
  rows    16-pixel tile rows of those blocks, before any X test
  vis     tile rows whose stretched span overlaps the clip window and the screen: one 8-byte
          pixel fetch each, two for a row of an 8bpp pair
  pix     screen pixels those rows cover after zoom and clipping: line-buffer writes
  tblrom  tile-table entries read from gfx2 ROM for visible rows (2 B each, consecutive
          within a block row)
  tblram  tile-table entries read from VRAM for visible rows (4 B each)

Findings:
- The worst attract line fetches 269 tile rows and writes 3058 pixels (frame 1436, line 141:
  the demo fight); the worst play line 243 rows and 2459 pixels. p99.9 is 245 / 2695 and
  221 / 2045. The load is the backgrounds: 16-tile-wide blocks drawn at half scale (xscale
  0x80) cover 128 pixels each but still fetch all 16 rows, and two or three of them tile
  the screen under the fighters, which are 8bpp pairs (two fetches a row).
- Per line a 320-pixel screen takes up to 269 fetches and 3058 writes: at one pixel a clock
  that is more than a 42 MHz line (2652 clocks) and 58% of an 84 MHz one (5304).
- At most 40 blocks cover a line (28 in play); 384 entries are walked once a frame.
- ROM tile tables: up to 141 entries a line; consecutive tiles of a block read consecutive
  entries, so an 8-byte granule serves four. VRAM tables: up to 100 a line, in BRAM.
- Unused entries are disabled (bit 15 clear), not parked off-screen as the SemiCom games do:
  rows and vis differ only by X clipping of blocks that straddle the screen edge.
